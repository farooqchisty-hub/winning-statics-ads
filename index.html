Winning Statics r9: the pattern in the sixteen reference ads, and the framework behind the twenty new ones

Date: 2026-09-07. Batch folder: adbatch-r9. Renders: GPT Image 2 via the MagicFit API. Embedded ads: ranked template library, read only, half from templates already shown in r2 to r8, half never shown.

1. What the sixteen reference ads have in common

Every one of them runs the same four beat sequence:

1. Hook. An imperative or declarative statement in heavy caps, 2 to 6 words, built on one of three verbs: send/link (URL IN. FINISHED ADS OUT. / SEND LINK. GET ADS.), stop (STOP BUILDING. / STOP PROMPTING.), or proven/winning (PROVEN ADS BY TONIGHT / SCALE ADS THAT ALREADY WON / WINNING ADS IN YOUR INBOX TONIGHT).

2. Proof object. Finished ad cards shown inside the ad, 3 to 9 of them, as the evidence that the service produces real output.

3. Offer line. A countable: 60 ads, 3 ratios, Meta copy, same day, 1,169 templates.

4. Ask. A pill button, almost always GET MY ADS.

The carrier metaphors vary (browser bar as printer, DM thread, DIY vs done for you split, dashboard on a phone, tablet, courier stack, skeleton hands for Halloween, contact wall with a blurred grid) but the reader's experience does not, because five things are held constant across all sixteen:

• One palette: near black, mint green, white. Sixteen ads read as one ad.

• One CTA verb.

• Thin UI line work and placeholder bars as the visual language. That is the "wireframe" feel: the picture describes an interface instead of showing a thing.

• Generic stock products (dark dropper bottles, black cans) rather than a believable brand, so the embedded ads look like lookalikes rather than library output.

• Copy that states the offer without saying anything a reader did not already assume.

3. The framework the twenty are built on

Same four beats, but each beat is rebuilt around a specific rule.

Stop. One hero object, rendered as a clean high end product render (glass, paper, brass, card), large, on a flat coloured ground. It is chosen for story appeal in Ogilvy's sense: the reader should want to know why it is there. Hourglass, contact sheet with grease pencil circles, split flap clock at 09:04, seed packet, passport stamps, receipt with PAID stamp, torn courier mailer, retired ad wearing a rosette. No two ads share a device.

Headline. One specific true thing, in Ogilvy's register: specificity over superlative (SEND IT AT 9:00. FIRST AD, 9:04.), news value (WE CIRCLED THE WINNERS.), a how to (HOW TO LAUNCH SIXTY ADS WITHOUT A BRIEF), an honest admission (LEMON. / A MACHINE MADE ALL FOUR.), a list of things you give up (Mercedes 230S structure), an ingredient claim (1,169 GO IN. YOUR SIXTY COME OUT., the Dove quarter cleansing cream structure). Hook cap height 300 to 380 px on the 2048 canvas so it survives at 400 px on a phone. Audience named in plain words above it, rotated: Shopify brands, DTC founders, ecommerce operators, media buyers, ecommerce teams.

Proof. Real library structures, never lookalikes. Every embedded ad carries its style's hard visual contract first and then the template's observed mechanism, applied to one of the eight fictional demo brands. Squared, front facing, full size. Half the templates are ones already rendered in r2 to r8 so approved styles carry through; half are fresh.

Offer. Countables only: 60 ads, ten styles, 1,169 layouts, three ratios, 24 hours or free, first ads in minutes, four Meta copy options a field, free rebuilds with no limit for wrong brand colour, broken text or wrong product, full commercial rights, $1.49 an ad on the 120 pack. Personalisation is implied as a bullet or tag ("built around your product"), not shouted in every hook.

Ask. The CTA verb changes with the concept: Start the clock, Show me mine, Get my sixty, Give them up, Send the link, Retire it tonight, Get every ratio, See the pack, Get all ten, Fill the bracket, Get them tonight, Plant it, Refill it, Open mine, Send the page.

Palette. Twenty different palettes, none of them the landing page mint. Aubergine and peach, darkroom black and film amber, cream and inspection red, bone and cobalt, butter yellow, forest and strike red, newsprint and cobalt, charcoal and gold, black and amber, passport navy and gold, slate and receipt white, white with the ads as colour, coral and ivory, electric blue, warm grey and tomato, ivory and oxblood, kraft and botanical green, pale grey and signal green, kraft and tape orange, white and highlighter yellow.

Canvas rules carried from r4 to r8. Zones tile the whole frame, 6 percent safe border, no text under 130 px, no long dashes, no performance figures inside any interface shown.

Edge QA (adbatch-r4/src/qa_edges.py) flags 19 of 20 on ink density in the 6 percent border. Every flag traces to a full-bleed colour band or a hero object (receipt, clock, film strip, mailer) bleeding to the frame, which the safe-area rule permits. No text, button or embedded ad card enters the border on any of the twenty; checked by eye at 2048.

Known soft spots worth a glance before spend: the empty task chair in 15 resembles a well known mesh office chair design; the three ratio frames in 10 are not exact 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16 proportions; competitor prices appear inside two embedded comparison ads (04, 05) as the template structure demands.
